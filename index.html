Three things to know first

1

What is BFAR?

The Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources. It is the government office to ask about collecting aquatic wildlife for research.

2

What is a permit?

A document that gives permission for an activity. A Gratuitous Permit (GP) can allow collection for research, with rules you must follow. Fees may still apply.

3

What should I do first?

Write down what you want to collect and where. If this is a school project, ask your teacher to help you answer the questions below.

Teacher help is a suggestion, not a new permit requirement. See the simple word guide ↓

What living things do you want to collect?

You can ask your teacher for help. If you do not know an answer, choose “I’m not sure”.

A scientific name is the name scientists use for a living thing. It often has two words. This website cannot identify a species or check its protection status.

Aquatic means living in water. CITES is an international agreement that controls trade in certain wildlife. Ask BFAR if you are unsure which group applies.

Protected wildlife has special legal rules. A familiar or common animal can still need checking. Do not guess from its name or appearance.

Collecting more than one kind? Include any special group that applies and ask BFAR to check the full list.

Tell us about you and your project.

High school students can use this guide. BFAR must confirm the correct rules and fee for the actual school project; we will not assume it is a college thesis.

A thesis or dissertation is a formal research project required for a degree. An ordinary school activity may follow a different process.

Funding means money used to pay for the project. Include foreign school or organization links, even if the collection happens in the Philippines.

Money from a government grant does not always make it a government project. Ask who started or carries out the research.

Tell us about the collection place.

CALABARZON includes Cavite, Laguna, Batangas, Rizal and Quezon. Two places in the same region do not automatically mean two regions.

Later, your work plan should include coordinates: map numbers that show the exact location. You can ask your teacher to help find them.

Examples include a protected park, an Indigenous community’s ancestral domain, or a place where someone holds private rights. BFAR can tell you whose permission is needed.

A few final checks.

Toxins are harmful substances. Some species with these properties need a special written agreement called an MOA. Ask your teacher or BFAR if you do not know.

Sending samples abroad may need other permits. The research permit alone may not cover this.

Detailed rules for each type of research

FAO 233 is a government rule about aquatic wildlife. Its numbered sections cover different types of research. You do not need to memorize the numbers. The step-by-step guide can help you find a starting point.

Which FAO 233 section might apply? Open the detailed rules
§ 22.1 Filipino researcher • no foreign funding

Required by FAO 233 Section 22.1. Baseline processing: BFAR Central Office under Section 22.7; see exceptions below.

  • Letter of application
  • Non-refundable application fee
  • Personal résumé
  • Brief research description or project proposal
  • Endorsement from the head of the affiliated institution; for an independent researcher, endorsement by a recognized expert from a research/academic institution or conservation organization
  • Site-appropriate prior consent or clearance: Indigenous Peoples’ FPIC, or concerned LGU clearance in consultation with FARMC; PAMB for protected areas, relevant rights holders and other agencies as applicable. Confirm which applies to each site; these are not interchangeable blanket clearances.
  • Affidavit of Undertaking; MOA with the Secretary instead where the specified toxin/pharmacological/commercial-property condition applies
  • GP issued by the Secretary if collection is from the wild, after completing requirements
§ 22.2 Foreign researcher, affiliation or funding

Required by FAO 233 Section 22.2. Baseline processing: BFAR Central Office under Section 22.7; see exceptions below.

  • Letter of application
  • Institutional profile or personal résumé
  • Brief research description or project proposal
  • Disclosure of the source of research funds
  • Local institution as research collaborator/counterpart and consent letter from its head
  • Site-appropriate prior consent or clearance: Indigenous Peoples’ FPIC, or concerned LGU clearance in consultation with FARMC; PAMB for protected areas, relevant rights holders and other agencies as applicable. Confirm which applies to each site; these are not interchangeable blanket clearances.
  • Non-refundable application fee
  • MOA between the Secretary and the proponent
  • GP issued by the Secretary for wild collection after completing requirements
§ 22.3 Conservation breeding or wildlife conservation project

Required by FAO 233 Section 22.3. Baseline processing: BFAR Central Office under Section 22.7; see exceptions below.

  • Letter of intent
  • Research/project proposal
  • Habitat restoration, protection and management plan for reintroduction/restocking of captive-bred individuals (captive-breeding projects only)
  • Acceptance letter from local collaborators (foreign applicants only)
  • Institutional or organizational profile
  • Endorsement from the institution/organization head
  • Disclosure of project funding source
  • Prior clearance under Rule 7.4 of Joint DA–DENR–PCSD AO No. 1; confirm site-specific authorities
  • Non-refundable application and processing fees
§ 22.4 Government collection for certified urgent concerns

Required by FAO 233 Section 22.4. Conditional exemption from the scientific-research rule; confirm eligibility with BFAR.

  • Certification by the government agency that collection addresses an urgent concern covered by Section 22.4
  • Prior notice to BFAR
  • Written research results and recommended plan of action to BFAR
§ 22.5 Government-initiated or implemented research

Required by FAO 233 Section 22.5. Baseline processing: Concerned BFAR Regional Office under Section 22.7; see exceptions below.

  • Letter of intent/application
  • Approved Work and Financial Plan
  • Research proposal
  • Site-appropriate prior consent or clearance: Indigenous Peoples’ FPIC, or concerned LGU clearance in consultation with FARMC; PAMB for protected areas, relevant rights holders and other agencies as applicable. Confirm which applies to each site; these are not interchangeable blanket clearances.
  • Grant or other agreement covering research funding
  • GP issued by the Regional Director for wild collection after completing requirements
§ 22.6 Student thesis or dissertation at a local institution

Required by FAO 233 Section 22.6. Baseline processing: Concerned BFAR Regional Office under Section 22.7; see exceptions below.

  • Letter of application
  • Certified true copy of the approved thesis proposal
  • Endorsement letters from the concerned Dean and thesis adviser
  • Site-appropriate prior consent or clearance: Indigenous Peoples’ FPIC, or concerned LGU clearance in consultation with FARMC; PAMB for protected areas, relevant rights holders and other agencies as applicable. Confirm which applies to each site; these are not interchangeable blanket clearances.
  • Non-refundable application fee for graduate students
  • Affidavit of Undertaking; MOA between the Secretary and the student where the specified toxin/pharmacological/commercial-property condition applies
  • GP issued by the Regional Director, or by the Secretary for species covered by MOA, for wild collection after completing requirements
Routing exceptions and current-office confirmation

Section 22.9: research covering two or more regions goes to Central Office. Section 22.7 sends 22.1–22.3 to Central Office and 22.4–22.6 to the concerned Regional Office, subject to the 22.6 MOA exception.

Threatened / protected / CITES: special review required — confirm the applicable permit and processing office with BFAR. Corals: the supplied BFAR 4A operational guidance refers cases to Central Office–FRLD–AWRS. Toxin / pharmacological / commercial-property cases: an MOA/Secretary-level route may apply.

The 2024 BFAR Citizen’s Charter describes some regional processing differently from the original Section 22.7. This guide shows the original FAO baseline and flags current delegation and office routing for confirmation; it does not silently treat the charter as an amendment.

PREPARE WELL

Write a clear plan for collecting.

BFAR 4A practical guidance
Use grams

State collection weight in grams. Add counts where helpful; use consistent quantities across documents.

Allow a realistic date range

Use a flexible proposed range for fieldwork. Collect only within the dates authorized by the issued permit.

Name every collector

Identify all personnel, collaborators and collectors involved, with their roles.

Locate every site

Give site names, municipality/province and coordinates, with species, methods and quantities per site.

The regional Annex A work plan is not the species-list Annex A of FAO 233. Confirm the accepted regional form; the download below is a preparation worksheet.

How to approach prior consent and clearances
Required by FAO 233 · applicable subsections

Site-appropriate prior consent or clearance: Indigenous Peoples’ FPIC, or concerned LGU clearance in consultation with FARMC; PAMB for protected areas, relevant rights holders and other agencies as applicable. Confirm which applies to each site; these are not interchangeable blanket clearances.

Identify each collection site first, then ask BFAR which authority must issue the consent or clearance. Do not substitute a generic LGU letter for Indigenous Peoples’ FPIC or PAMB clearance where applicable.

Common issues to check before sending
  • Scientific names and quantities match across the letter, proposal and work plan.
  • Dean and adviser endorsements are included for the student pathway.
  • Scans are readable; signatures and certifications required for your pathway are present.
  • All sites, collectors, funding sources and special species concerns are disclosed.
  • Use the correct current BFAR format; illustrative downloads are not approved forms.

WORDS MADE SIMPLE

What does that word mean?

You can come back here whenever you see an unfamiliar word.

Scientific name

The name scientists use for a living thing. It often has two words.

Species

A kind of living thing. Use its scientific name to avoid confusion.

GP — Gratuitous Permit

A permit for activities such as collecting aquatic wildlife for research. Fees may still apply.

FAO 233

Fisheries Administrative Order No. 233: the government rule used in this guide.

Endorsement letter

A letter from the required school or organization official supporting your application.

Certified true copy

A copy certified as matching the original. Ask the office which certification it accepts.

Affidavit of Undertaking

A formal sworn statement about commitments. Ask BFAR for the correct form and signing instructions.

MOA — Memorandum of Agreement

A written agreement between named parties. Some cases need one with the Secretary of Agriculture.

Clearance / consent

Permission from the authority, community or rights holder that applies to the place. BFAR can help identify the right process.

Inventory

A list of what you collected, with amounts and collection details.

Order of Payment

The document that tells the Cashier what you need to pay.

Records and FRMS

BFAR office names used in the submission steps. Use the email addresses shown; you do not need to memorize the initials.

LGU / FARMC / PAMB

LGU means local government unit. FARMC is a fisheries and aquatic resources management council. PAMB is a protected area management board. BFAR will tell you which needs to be involved.

FPIC

Free and Prior Informed Consent: a specific consent process involving Indigenous Peoples where applicable. A generic letter does not replace it.

NFRDI

National Fisheries Research and Development Institute. FAO 233 requires a copy of your final research results to be sent there too.

04 / QUICK ANSWERS

Questions you might have.

Short answers to common questions. These follow the topics in the supplied brief and available chat, rather than quoting the missing applicant conversations.

23 questions

I’m in high school. Can I use this guide?

Yes. Ask your teacher to help you describe the project. Then ask BFAR which permit, papers and fee apply. A school activity is not automatically a college thesis under Section 22.6.

What if I do not know an answer?

Choose “I’m not sure”. Write down the question and ask your teacher or BFAR. Do not guess the protection status of a plant or animal.

Does this website send my application?

No. It only helps you prepare. You still need to send your documents to the correct office. The email button opens your email app; you must add the files yourself.

Can I use only the common name?

Add the scientific name if you know it. Common names can mean different things. Ask your teacher or BFAR to check the name and any protection rules. This website cannot look them up.

Do all researchers follow Section 22.5?

No. Section 22.5 is for research started or carried out by government. Other researchers may follow Sections 22.1, 22.2 or 22.3. The fee alone does not decide the rule.

Do protected or CITES-listed species always go to Central Office?

Do not assume that. Ask BFAR which permit and office apply. These living things need special review. A research GP may not be enough.

Where do I ask about coral?

The supplied BFAR 4A instructions refer coral cases to BFAR Central Office’s aquatic wildlife team: FRLD–AWRS. This is regional office guidance, not a blanket rule quoted from FAO 233.

What if we collect in two regions?

FAO 233 Section 22.9 says to file with BFAR Central Office. Two towns in the same region do not automatically count as two regions.

How much do students pay?

The supplied regional totals are ₱20 for Filipino undergraduate students and ₱120 for graduate students, including Master’s and doctorate. Other researchers generally pay ₱200. High school projects and special cases should ask BFAR to confirm the fee. These amounts are not a bill.

Can I pay the two fees together?

The supplied BFAR 4A instructions allow one payment for convenience. The application fee and GP fee are still separate parts. Get an Order of Payment first. Do not pay through this website.

Do I send one PDF or several?

Either is allowed in the supplied regional instructions: one combined PDF or separate readable PDFs. For Region IV-A, send TO region4arecords@bfar.da.gov.ph and put frms.4a@bfar.da.gov.ph in CC.

What do I put in the Annex A work plan?

List the scientific names, amounts in grams, collection places and map coordinates, methods, possible dates, and everyone who will collect. A proposed date does not give permission to collect before approval.

Is this work plan the same as Annex A in FAO 233?

No. The regional “Annex A work plan” is an application attachment. The Annex A inside FAO 233 is a species list. Ask BFAR for its current work-plan form.

Who signs the supporting letters for a thesis?

Section 22.6 lists letters from the concerned Dean and thesis adviser. Ask BFAR before combining them into one letter. For a different school project, first confirm which rules apply.

Who should be listed as a collector?

List everyone who will collect samples, including helpers and collaborators, with their roles. Tell BFAR about changes and follow the names and conditions in the issued permit.

Can we collect while waiting for approval?

Do not treat an application or payment as permission. Get the required authorization first. Check the approved dates, places, species and amounts before collecting.

What if we accidentally collect something else?

Include it in the Sampling Report and Inventory under the supplied regional guidance. Tell BFAR promptly about problems and follow the permit. Listing it later does not make an unauthorized collection allowed.

When do we send the collection report?

The supplied regional instruction is within 60 days after the last collection day. Send the Sampling Report and Inventory. Check the permit for other deadlines or earlier reporting duties.

Where do we send the finished research?

For Region IV-A, email Records and CC FRMS using the addresses on this page. FAO 233 Section 22.10 also requires results to BFAR and NFRDI. Ask how to send NFRDI its copy; do not assume the Records email reaches NFRDI.

What happens after we send our papers?

BFAR checks the documents and may ask you to correct or add something. Section 22.8 says incomplete applications cannot be accepted. Ask the office about payment and when the permit can be claimed. This guide does not promise a processing time.

Does government money make it a government project?

Not always. Ask who started or carries out the project. A government grant alone does not automatically put a private or school project under Section 22.5.

What if we buy the samples?

Tell BFAR where the samples come from. Ask about permit coverage and documents for getting or moving them. Do not assume bought or farmed samples need no permission.

What about Palawan, sending samples abroad or making products?

Ask BFAR for specific advice. These cases can involve other offices or rules. Do not assume a research GP covers them.

No matching questions. Try a broader word, or contact BFAR for guidance.
